# Day 6: Retail Analytics Platform Storage Decision

**Bronze:** GZIP Parquet (slower, but compresses smaller, and this layer is rarely queried directly, it's mostly there for reprocessing and audits, not day-to-day BI), kept raw, not cleaned yet. Partition by the date it was loaded on, since that matches how we would re-run or check a specific day's data, and keeps the folder count small as new data comes in daily.

**Silver:** Snappy Parquet (faster to read/write, since it's touched more often in this layer, such as the ETL transformations like Type 1/2 SCD logic and upserts), since the data is cleaned and typed now, and Parquet keeps those types instead of losing them like CSV or JSON does. Partition by order year and month for the `orders` and `payments` tables, since that's a good amount of folders and matches how people usually filter by date. `order_items` does not have its own date column, but during the Silver transformation it's joined to `orders` on `order_id` to bring in `order_date`, so it gets partitioned by year and month too. `customers`, `products`, and `categories` have no date column and are small to moderate in size, so they stay unpartitioned as low-cardinality tables don't need the small-file overhead of partitioning.

**Gold:** Snappy Parquet, still the best for fast reads for BI analysts and finance. `FactSales` and `FactPayments` partition by year and month, matching the main way people filter sales and payment reports. `DimDate`, `DimCustomer`, `DimProduct`, and `DimChannel` stay unpartitioned, they're small dimension tables, and partitioning them would create more small files than it would ever save in read time.
